# Chapter 28: Choose investments under uncertainty

This is a self-study kit for the book's reader-facing Chapter 28. All Harbor facts and counts are invented teaching material. No private workplace data, paid account or AI tool is required.

## Work the paper case first

Use the separate paper case: smaller repair $6,000 setup plus $2,000/year for two years; optional draft aid $12,000 setup plus $3,000/year incremental. Across two years there are 6,000 eligible questions, four baseline minutes, one review minute, adoption of 50% or 75%, and an illustrative $40/hour capacity value. A $10,000 vendor demo equates receipt with approval and fails R7. Calculate direct costs and potential hours; apply fit before cost ranking. No number is a quote or observed benefit.

1. Read the chapter, then spend about 30 minutes on your own answer.
2. Copy [worksheet.csv](worksheet.csv), fill the fields and retain your assumptions. Use a spreadsheet or plain text; blank template cells are for your work.
3. Challenge your first answer: Are you comparing incremental with incremental, or mixing the aid component with a complete option? Would the same hours be counted as both cash and capacity? Which assumption reverses the recommendation?
4. Revise before opening the [worked answer and rubric](worked-answer.md). Compare reasoning and evidence boundaries rather than copying a preferred conclusion.
5. Repeat with a safe fictional or sanitized case of your own. More than one conclusion can earn full credit when the reasoning is justified.

## Deliverable

One same-scope options memo, reproducible arithmetic, two sensitivity scenarios, explicit cash/capacity distinction and a staged recommendation.

AI may propose or challenge from permitted inputs. People validate meaning, owners, funding and commitments. An invented source, automatic approval or missing receiver does not become evidence because a model says so. Keep a paper/human route usable.
