# Chapter 14: Lead Across Functions — worked answers

All examples are fictional. No Harbor cross-functional acceptance, policy approval, support capacity, release or learner outcome is claimed.

## Teach-back

A sent handoff proves an upstream event occurred. An accepted handoff means a receiving owner has the information, authority, capacity and working route to carry out an agreed next step. A shared outcome describes what the learner can do; local outputs are components of that result. A facilitator can surface a decision and track dissent, but does not acquire policy, security, support or release authority by running a meeting.

## Worked chapter arithmetic and decision

At invented Harbor rates, twelve unresolved contacts arrive per day and Support can complete eight. Net unfinished demand is `12 − 8 = 4/day`. With zero initial backlog and constant rates over five days, `4 × 5 = 20` remain. This says nothing about observed demand or individual performance. The chapter's proposed narrow wording/handoff correction follows Chapter 13's conditional 48 implementation hours plus separately reserved supporting work; support, policy and release acceptance are separate. A broader routing feature or autonomous case action is deferred. A simpler static text/form path may beat the assisted path if it gives the learner the right next step with lower full-service cost.

## Separate paper packet

Thirty unresolved handoffs/day minus twenty-four daily completions = six unfinished/day. After three days from zero backlog, `6 × 3 = 18` unfinished. Do not mix these numbers with the chapter's 12/8/5 example or Chapter 13's weekly engineering capacity. Finance's digital-message count does not prove resolution.

One acceptable three-row ledger:

| Interface | Owner and acceptance | Escalation / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| R7 meaning | Policy owner confirms current approved source that submitted/received does not mean approved; Engineering uses the version in a negative case. | Missing source or conflicting language stops release; policy owner decides, sponsor changes scope/date if needed. |
| Receiving route | Support owner demonstrates a route a person can find and use, trained receiver, permitted context and sustainable capacity. Accessibility/user research includes affected people through an authorized method. | 30/day vs 24/day gap requires narrowed exposure, improved self-service, approved capacity or later date; demonstrate with route test and queue observation. |
| Release | Release authority approves only after policy, support and security/access gates pass. Engineering provides clean/dirty tests and Operations a stop/fallback route. | Failed acceptance blocks exposure. Sponsor chooses tradeoff; do not count a sent handoff as accepted. |

A defensible first option is a smaller static or staff-reviewed message correction with existing human channel, after the receiving route is proved. Another is a later launch with funded receiving capacity and a bounded observation period. A learner may argue for an urgent accessible correction sooner if an authorized alternative is already staffed; require evidence, owner and documented consequence. No option may give the assistant independent send, policy or learner-record authority.

Hear from people who cannot find or use the default phone/digital route, without inventing their testimony or using inaccessible research methods. AI may draft a redacted journey map or check the arithmetic from approved synthetic inputs; a human verifies sources, owners and affected groups. A falsifying test is an authorized journey task showing that people can see the message but cannot find or complete a usable next step, or queue age rising while digital interactions are counted as resolved.

## Transfer rubric (12 points)

Award one point each for:

1. Defines an affected-person outcome and traces the whole path, not only software delivery.
2. Names at least four necessary teams or distinct authorities without assuming one can override all.
3. Distinguishes offer/sent event from receiver acceptance with a concrete test.
4. Makes workload or capacity visible without inventing an observed baseline.
5. Assigns policy meaning, access, support operation and release decisions to legitimate owners.
6. Records dissent or an unresolved assumption and shows how it changes the proposed decision.
7. Gives a dated escalation/reopening condition when acceptance fails.
8. Compares a simpler non-AI option and an AI-assisted option on outcome and total cost.
9. Identifies an underrepresented affected group and an ethical way to obtain evidence.
10. Gives AI a bounded source, purpose, human review and enforceable permission boundary.
11. Uses paired local and whole-journey signals, including correction or queue effects.
12. Walks trigger, propagation, detection, containment, owner, recovery and residual effects.

## Common wrong answers and feedback

- **“Engineering shipped, so the dependency is done.”** Ask the learner to show the receiver's acceptance test and who can work the queue.
- **“Finance saved money because fewer contacts were logged.”** Ask where unresolved work went and count support, corrections and affected-person delay.
- **“One sponsor approval covers policy, access and accessibility.”** A sponsor can choose resources and scope; domain decisions need actual authority and evidence.
- **“AI can interview absent learners and summarize their needs.”** An agent cannot create direct stakeholder evidence. Arrange authorized research and label unknowns meanwhile.

Accept different scope, date or resource choices when they preserve the R7 boundary, require real receiving acceptance, expose tradeoffs and specify evidence that could change the recommendation.
