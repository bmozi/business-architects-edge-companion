# Chapter 27: Connect strategy to execution — worked answers

This guide accompanies Chapter 27's paper and transfer tasks. The 12-message and six-case packet is invented, separate from all other Harbor examples. Do not present it as a baseline, before/after comparison, sampling frame, deployment result or outcome study.

## Teaching purpose and use

The learner should be able to defend each link from an initiative to a stakeholder outcome, mark the weak links as hypotheses, identify who must operate the change after delivery, define measures that can be challenged, and change the plan when evidence or a receiving team's constraint warrants it. Suggested use: 30 minutes individual work, 15 minutes paired critique, and 15 minutes to revise the trace. For independent study, the learner should first write a response before reading this guide.

## Paper packet: worked answer

**Arithmetic.** Nine of twelve delivered messages use current approved wording: 9/12 = 75% **of those twelve reviewed messages**. Four of six sampled unresolved cases reached a named receiver with agreed context: 4/6 = 66⅔% **of those six sampled cases**. The denominators describe different populations and behaviors. Neither percentage estimates Harbor-wide quality or learner outcomes. No pre-change sample exists, so neither supports a change-over-time statement. A current approved wording rate does not establish that learners understood the wording; receipt by a named receiver does not establish resolution.

| Packet item | Best classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Page deployed | Deliverable | A completed project output; operation and benefit still need observation. |
| Message uses current wording | Operating indicator | Shows whether one designed behavior appears in a defined sample; it is not comprehension or outcome. |
| Case reaches receiver | Handoff indicator | Tests a part of the operating route; acceptance, response quality and capacity remain separate. |
| Learner reaches authorized start | Stakeholder outcome | This is the valued later condition, subject to cohort, time and authorization definitions. |

A defensible five-link trace is:

1. **Outcome:** admitted learners reach an authorized start with usable guidance; outcome owner must define cohort and time window.
2. **Journey stage:** understand current requirements/status and know the next action.
3. **Capability:** explain status accurately and route unresolved cases to help.
4. **Operating change:** policy-approved R7 wording is delivered across relevant channels, while a staffed accessible path carries an exception to an accepting receiver with required context.
5. **Temporary initiative:** review/correct the page and other active messages; agree and test the route. An assistant is a later option, not a necessary fifth link.

Place question marks at least on **operating change → clearer learner understanding** and **understanding/route → authorized starts**. Other good uncertainty markers include whether G1 occurs often enough to matter, whether all channels adopt the change, whether the receiving function has capacity, and whether learners can use the channel.

The continuing **outcome owner** is an accountable business sponsor or leader who can review evidence and change operating priorities after project close. The **policy owner** approves status meaning; **support and admissions operations** accept and sustain the route; the **project lead** can own delivery but cannot substitute for all of those roles. Accept different role titles if decision rights are explicit and plausible.

A strong **guardrail** is the number or rate of messages that mistakenly equate receipt with satisfaction, or unresolved cases stranded beyond an agreed interval. Define its source and denominator before interpreting it. A simpler next action is to correct any known misleading wording under policy approval and establish a human receiver with an accessible route before funding new automation. A bounded AI evaluation may be worth considering if, after those repairs, a permitted sample shows recurring approved-source explanation work that staff-reviewed drafts could handle, sources are current, review is practical and a fallback exists. The packet does not establish any of those conditions.

The project cannot be said to have improved starts because the packet contains **no start data, no prior comparison, no comprehension evidence and no credible causal design**. Even a later change in starts would need examination of other changes. The immediate finding is narrower: three of twelve reviewed messages lack current approved wording and two of six sampled unresolved cases lack the agreed handoff, if the packet's classifications are accurate. Each should be investigated by an authorized owner without generalizing its frequency.

## Facilitation probes and common wrong answers

Ask: “Can the project manager close the delivery work while the benefit remains unproved?” Yes, if delivery criteria are met; the ongoing outcome owner must still track and act on the benefit hypothesis. Ask: “Which arrow in your trace has the least evidence?” The answer should be specific, not “we need more data.” Ask: “What would the receiving team need to accept a case?” The learner should name context, authority, a usable contact path and capacity, then verify rather than invent the contract.

Common wrong answer 1: “The page is deployed, so strategy is executed.” Feedback: deployment is a deliverable. Ask who uses it, whether the right wording appears across channels, whether unresolved cases reach an owner, and whether the intended outcome was observed.

Common wrong answer 2: “75% success proves we need AI.” Feedback: the numerator checks only current wording within twelve selected messages. It does not identify the cause, frequency across Harbor, relative effectiveness of a simpler repair, or whether AI would improve anything. Repair a known false message under authority, then compare viable interventions against defined needs.

Common wrong answer 3: “The remaining 25% caused fewer starts.” Feedback: no start data or counterfactual exists. A defect can warrant correction without supporting that causal or quantitative claim.

Common wrong answer 4: “Support owns the outcome because it owns the route.” Feedback: support owns a crucial operating contribution. Name a leader who can coordinate across policy, admissions, support and funding and keep review alive after the project ends.

## Transfer-task rubric (12 points)

Score each dimension 0, 1 or 2. Give 2 for concrete, internally consistent evidence; 1 for a partial or vague entry; 0 for absent or contradictory work. Do not convert the total into a claim about job readiness or actual institutional performance.

| Dimension | What earns 2 points |
|---|---|
| Outcome and trace | Defines a stakeholder outcome and a coherent journey → capability → gap → operating change → initiative/benefit chain. |
| Evidence and assumptions | Distinguishes observed or supplied evidence from hypotheses, marks uncertain causal links and says what evidence would revise them. |
| Ownership | Separates delivery, ongoing benefit/outcome, policy/specialist and receiving-operation authority; identifies affected people. |
| Measure quality | Gives useful leading, lagging and guardrail measures with population, numerator/denominator or observation method, source, cadence and limitations. |
| Alternatives and AI authority | Compares at least one simpler option; bounds any AI input, provenance, access, human validation, permitted action and manual fallback. Full credit is possible when AI is rejected with reason. |
| Adaptive decision | Has a reversible next step, decision gate, stop/revise condition and a plausible green-project/flat-outcome failure response. |

Feedback should point to the first unsupported link and ask the learner to repair it. A sanitized or fully fictional transfer case is acceptable; do not reward privileged workplace access. The government's benefits and assurance guidance informs the exercise design but does not validate the fictional Harbor trace or prescribe a single organizational role chart.

## ArchiMate 4 small-view exercise — worked answer

The four statements are: the Application Component realizes the Service; the application-context Service serves the business-context Process; the Process realizes the Capability; the Process realizes the Requirement. Dashed line/hollow triangle means realization toward what is realized; solid/open arrow means serving toward the functionality user. The model is an original proposed target, not a test report.

Restore the capability name to “Explain current status and next action accurately,” keeping the tool in an Application Component. Restore the serving direction from the wording Service to the explanation Process. The reversed pair may express legal but wrong business meaning; distinguish semantic agreement from type validity.

Information question: which approved source/version governs receipt and how is it kept distinct from satisfaction? Organization question: who may explain the rule, and who accepts unresolved case types/hours/context? Checks include Policy-approved negative cases, source binding, permitted access, real receiver acceptance, usable fallback and observed comprehension where permitted. Component/service realization does not establish those controls, policy authority, send permission or learner results. A model that omits them must not silently imply they exist.

Award ten points: four correct relationship sentences; two correct symbol meanings; two repairs; one meaningful omitted information/organization check; one explanation of model assertion versus evidence and authority. A learner can reasonably retain a plain table if the formal view adds no decision value.
