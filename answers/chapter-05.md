# Chapter 5: Build enterprise AI capabilities — worked answers

## Cost calculations

**Separate team services:** setup is `3 × $6,000 = $18,000`. Runtime is `3 × $1,200 = $3,600/month`. Operation/review is `30 hours × $75/hour = $2,250/month`. Total monthly modeled cost is `$5,850`; first-year modeled total is `$18,000 + 12 × $5,850 = $88,200`.

**Thin shared service:** setup is `$24,000`. Monthly runtime is `$1,800`, platform operation is `32 × $75 = $2,400`, and domain review is `18 × $75 = $1,350`. Total monthly modeled cost is `$5,550`; first-year modeled total is `$24,000 + 12 × $5,550 = $90,600`.

**Purchased capability:** setup is `$9,000`. Monthly license is `$3,000`, upkeep is `16 × $75 = $1,200`, and domain review is `$1,350`. Total monthly modeled cost is `$5,550`; first-year modeled total is `$9,000 + 12 × $5,550 = $75,600`.

Shared first-year cost exceeds local by $2,400. Purchased is $15,000 below shared in the model. Shared has $300 lower monthly modeled cost than local but $6,000 higher setup. None proves realized savings or suitability. These are invented inputs, nominal twelve-month arithmetic, incomplete cost scope, and not functionally verified alternatives; labor valuation is capacity consumption rather than a promised payroll change. Real costs, outcomes, and risks require evidence.

## Boundary critique

1. Cheapest means suitable: business/procurement/technical owners must verify required controls, behavior, service, terms, uncertainty and exit rather than assume equivalence. In the added exercise fact, one green status cannot yet represent receipt and approval separately. Policy owner confirms the distinction; procurement/engineering obtain a supported product demonstration for unresolved and approved cases. Until then the purchased candidate cannot be ranked as a viable cheaper option. An adapter is a new proposed design, with Harbor owning its semantic mapping, tests, monitoring, vendor-change response and incident handling; its cost is not included in $75,600. A buyer may show an alternative supported configuration and re-enter the fit comparison. No real vendor failure is implied.
2. One unrestricted data-source credential: technical/security and domain/data owners must define and enforce request/task/context authorization. A shared technical service need not imply one unrestricted data identity; model routing credentials and data-source permissions also differ.
3. Question-only cache: technical owner must evaluate whether context, data scope, evidence/configuration validity and permission changes affect reuse. Same wording is not proof that the same answer may be disclosed.
4. Platform approves every answer: domain/policy owner retains meaning and business decision authority; shared infrastructure cannot accept all task consequences. Evaluation/operations capacity and actual responsibility agreement required.

Other valid critiques: hidden ongoing review, lack of shutdown/incident response, unlimited retries, unsupported provider fallbacks, and budget alerts mistaken for enforced limits. Accept any four distinct supported issues with owners/evidence. The fit conclusion and adapter ownership are required follow-through on the supplied vendor scenario; they may be included in the cheapest-means-suitable critique rather than counted as extra separate issues.

Smaller step: define one task/service card and compare verified existing/purchased capability with a minimal shared/local path using approved data and evaluation. Do not buy or implement a universal platform solely because this exercise supplies a cost table.

If an available replacement is unapproved for the data, do not route there automatically. Use an approved template/manual path, reject/defer according to the contract, or obtain the appropriate review/authorization. Old-version rollback might be impossible; preserve incident/change records and communicate the impact through accountable owners.

## Transfer: Harbor example

Consumer/outcome: onboarding staff need faithful explanations of one approved requirement. Task: propose explanation with applicable source/context, not approve eligibility. Inputs: validated caller/task/workspace and approved R7 evidence. No broad live-record access, writes, waivers, or independent outreach. Configuration: model/instruction/retrieval/evidence/policy/evaluation identifiers. Limits: agreed latency/usage/retry behavior, unavailable path. Owners: platform technical operation, domain policy meaning and evaluation, receiving support, and business progression decision.

Alternatives: reviewed template/existing tools, thin shared model/evidence/usage path, managed product with verified integration/isolation. Access test: same question under distinct permitted contexts and revoked access. Change test: replacement model must not turn unresolved into approval. Outage test: provider unavailable invokes approved non-generative/manual route. Retirement: capability no longer supports outcome or cannot meet necessary boundaries; migrate consumers/revoke access according to agreed retention and contracts.

Proposed transfer rubric: useful service card, 2; genuine alternatives and uncertainty, 2; three meaningful tests, 2; accountable operation and guardrails, 2; fallback, exit and no invented agreements, 2. Total: 10.

Common misconceptions: authentication proves isolation; centralization creates domain expertise; budget alert caps spending; all model endpoints are interchangeable; purging cache reverses disclosure; using AI to draft a contract creates agreement. Ask which implemented control or accountable commitment supports each claim.
