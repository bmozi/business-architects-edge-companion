# Chapter 16: Align Business and Technology — worked answers

All packets are fictional. No Harbor strategy, architecture decision, accessible-user result, R7 test, release or business benefit is observed.

## Teach-back

Business–technology alignment is a revisable trace: desired human/business outcome → capability → requirements and quality attributes → technical and operating choice → evidence and correction. A faster deployment, passing build or lower cost per generated draft can support a service, but it does not independently prove that a learner understands the next step. Leading, lagging, guardrail and delivery-health measures answer different questions. Every measure needs a definition, unit, source, time window and owner; proportions need a numerator and denominator, while durations need start and end events.

## Worked chapter case

A first candidate is approved static/staff-reviewed R7 wording plus a tested existing human route, subject to actual policy, support, security and release acceptance. A staff-reviewed AI draft path remains a conditional comparator for cases the simpler path cannot serve. Autonomous send/write/case/eligibility action is outside the decision. Required quality attributes: semantic correctness (receipt≠satisfaction with current source digest), accessibility, per-learner authorization, fallback/recovery and changeability. AI can draft synthetic fixtures or the decision trace from approved material; it cannot approve its own test oracle or grant domain authority.

The chapter's invented case-mix example: initial routine `18/20 = 90%`, exception `4/10 = 40%`, total `22/30 = 73.333...%`. Later routine `36/40 = 90%`, exception `4/10 = 40%`, total `40/50 = 80%`. Aggregate increase is about **6.7 percentage points**, but neither task-type rate improved. The routine share rose from 20/30 to 40/50. This does not show a causal AI effect or a population quality rate. Report counts, sample selection, rule version and affected channels before drawing conclusions.

A separate invented cost-error thought experiment lowers per-draft cost from $2.00 to $1.20, a 40% reduction, while a tiny convenience sample changes from 1/20 wrong statuses to 4/20. The counts are a detection concern, not precise error-rate estimates or proof that the cost change caused errors. Stop/narrow exposure when a wrong R7 statement or access violation appears; count review, correction and affected-person cost before claiming savings.

## Separate paper packet answer

Initial routine `8/10 = 80%`, exception `2/5 = 40%`, total `(8+2)/(10+5) = 10/15 = 66.666...%`. Later routine `24/30 = 80%`, exception `2/5 = 40%`, total `(24+2)/(30+5) = 26/35 = 74.2857...%`. Apparent aggregate gain is about **7.6 percentage points**. The within-type rates are unchanged; routine tasks account for 10/15 initially and 30/35 later. The executive claim “AI improved correctness” does not follow. Task mix, selection, a rule change, reviewer learning, channel shifts or other concurrent work could explain the aggregate. Small samples and no randomized comparison further limit inference.

One acceptable mini-map: goal = learner distinguishes receipt from satisfaction and can reach a next step; capability = current policy explanation plus human route; requirement = correct R7 wording, accessible path, per-learner access, current digest; option = approved static/staff-reviewed path first; owner = Policy for meaning, Support for receiving, Security for access, release authority for exposure; evidence = approved negative case, authorized user task, queue and correction observations. Leading condition = share of planned R7 changes with current policy digest and dirty-path test (numerator compliant reviewed changes, denominator all R7 changes in window, source review/CI). Lagging check = proportion of eligible study participants who state correct next step (define cohort, consent, incomplete tasks and coding). Guardrail = any independently verified false satisfaction statement or cross-learner access, with stop and authorized correction. Delivery health = DORA measure such as lead time alongside change fail and rework, never a learner outcome proxy. If static path fails important varied tasks in a comparable study and the AI path improves them with sustainable review/receiving capacity, revise the recommendation.

## Transfer rubric (12 points)

Award one point each for:

1. Names an affected-person outcome and capability rather than a technology output.
2. Traces a plausible causal chain with explicit assumptions that could fail.
3. Compares a simple/non-AI option with a bounded assisted option and an excluded action where appropriate.
4. Specifies current policy/data contracts, semantic tests and change/version propagation.
5. Accounts for accessibility, privacy and a receiving human route.
6. Names operating owner, staff review, stop/fallback and correction path.
7. Defines a leading condition with numerator, denominator, source and window.
8. Defines a learner-facing lagging check and meaningful segments/missingness.
9. Defines a guardrail that can stop exposure plus a distinct delivery-health measure.
10. Handles task mix, sampling and concurrent-change confounding without claiming causality.
11. Gives AI permitted inputs, human verification and enforced limits.
12. Identifies a sponsor review choice and conditions for changing or retiring the architecture.

## Common wrong answers and feedback

- **“Correctness went from 66.7% to 74.3%, so AI worked.”** Compute routine and exception rates separately; both stayed the same. Ask how cases were selected.
- **“More deployments prove alignment.”** Deployment is a delivery signal. Ask what learner task changed and what study/operating evidence supports it.
- **“The cheaper model lowers cost to serve.”** Include review, wrong guidance, support and correction costs; cost per draft is an incomplete denominator.
- **“The accessible link exists, so the route is ready.”** Test whether a person can find/use it and whether a trained receiver accepts the work.

Accept a different first architecture when the learner makes the outcome, owners, evidence, uncertainties and safe operational boundary defensible.
