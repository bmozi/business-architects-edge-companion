# Chapter 2: Explain simply and question honestly — worked answers

## Revise the promise

Example: “We have received your documents. An authorized staff member still needs to check whether they satisfy this requirement. Receiving them does not confirm eligibility or permission to start. If you need help, use the service's verified support route.”

The exercise gives no actual URL; a response should refer to the existing route without inventing contact details. Production wording must provide that real route appropriately. Shorter wording can receive full credit if it preserves receipt versus pending review and does not imply approval. It is also defensible to remove unnecessary concepts if doing so cannot imply permission; explain the reasoning.

Wrongly combined meanings: receipt of documents versus a determination that they satisfy a requirement; satisfaction of one requirement versus eligibility/permission to start. The scenario establishes only receipt and pending staff review. It supplies no start date, review deadline, final result, or waiver authority.

Question for policy owner: “Which conditions establish that the documents satisfy this requirement, and who may make that determination?” Question for Operations: “What should a learner do if the pending review remains unresolved, and which team can accept that request?” Other open questions earn credit when they address a real unknown rather than force agreement.

Changed scenario: one document was received but later identified as unreadable. The explanation must not imply that the requirement is satisfied. Another valid variant is a completed review that still leaves a separate start condition unresolved. Do not invent a new permission in the variant without identifying its responsible source.

## Proposed rubric: 10 points

- 3: rewrite is understandable, preserves pending review, and does not promise eligibility/start.
- 2: identifies two mistaken conceptual jumps with reasoning.
- 2: genuine policy and operations questions addressing different responsibilities.
- 2: changed case tests the actual boundary, with a justified expected action.
- 1: no invented dates, guarantees, contact details, authority, or results.

Common errors and feedback: “You're almost approved” invents probability; ask what supports it. “The AI will finish the review” grants authority not supplied. “You'll hear back tomorrow” invents a deadline. “Contact support” without acknowledging the pending status may leave the central misconception intact. A long rewrite is not automatically better; ask which words help the intended action.

## Transfer task: worked Harbor answer

Term: ready. Audience: learner support deciding what to tell a learner. Decision: whether the message describes receipt, requirement satisfaction, or permission to start. Plain explanation: the service should distinguish what it knows from what an authorized function still needs to decide. Analogy: a package delivered to an office is not the same as its contents being accepted; limit: actual policy may depend on additional rules beyond receipt/inspection.

Card source: authoritative requirements and an authorized recorded status, with defined freshness and ownership. Example acceptance condition: when receipt is recorded but review remains pending, the explanation may acknowledge receipt and give the approved next step, but must not imply requirement satisfaction or permission to start. A reviewer can check this against an ordinary case and a misleading “ready” counterexample. Missing/conflicting information follows an approved routing process. The assistant explains but cannot determine eligibility or change records. Open question: “Which of these status distinctions is needed for a learner's next safe action?”

Legitimate objection: Operations cannot accept every uncertain request. Revision: narrow the first scope to a defined requirement, distinguish missing material from conflicting interpretation, and agree on receiving owners before a pilot. This is a hypothetical plan, not a claim of agreement or measured improvement.

Teach-back: “The message sounds confident but the authoritative status is unresolved; what may support tell the learner?” Expected reasoning: retain uncertainty, do not treat generated wording as permission, follow the authorized next step. A reviewer should check meaning, authority, audience comprehension, and operation rather than fluency alone.

## Transfer scoring proposal: 10 points

Audience and decision 2; accurate explanation and analogy limit 2; source/authority/uncertainty 2; genuine objection and revision 2; checkable acceptance condition 1; changed scenario with an owned next step 1. Accept justified non-AI templates and other defensible alternatives.

Facilitation: let learners answer before showing the key. Ask which fact supports each promise. If they cannot answer a conceptual question, explain or provide a concrete example rather than repeatedly questioning without instruction. Neither disagreement nor low confidence is proof of poor reasoning.
