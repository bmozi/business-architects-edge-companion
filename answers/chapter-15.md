# Chapter 15: Partner with Executives — worked answers

All examples are fictional and separate from other chapter cost worksheets. No Harbor or WGU executive decision, budget, policy acceptance, learner benefit or staffing commitment is claimed.

## Teach-back

An executive sponsor may decide strategy, scope, priority or investment within actual delegated authority. Policy meaning, security access, receiving-team operation and release exposure retain their own accountable owners. An architect influences through a fair choice and evidence, not by treating a persuasive deck as permission. A decision brief is successful when the sponsor can answer yes, no or wait and know what each answer changes.

## Worked chapter arithmetic and recommendation

The invented 90-day Option B direct cost is `$12,000 + 3 × $3,000 = $21,000`. Option C is `$38,000 + 3 × $6,000 = $56,000`. Incremental C over B is `$35,000`. Option A's `$0 incremental` omits existing costs and is not a claim that doing nothing is costless. Autonomous action is unpriced and excluded because its authority and receiver contract are absent.

A deliberately generous labor-only upper bound is `12 contacts/day × 90 days × 8 minutes ÷ 60 = 144 hours`; at `$50/hour`, that is `$7,200` in illustrative *planning capacity value*, not cash or a forecast. At 12 completely eliminated contacts/day for 90 days, `35,000 × 60 ÷ (12 × 90 × 50) = 38.888... minutes/contact` for labor-only parity with the $35,000 increment. The assumed eight minutes is much smaller. This does not rule out real learner/accessibility benefit or a different use case; it rules out claiming the invented labor scenario alone pays for C. It is also an upper bound based on a fictitious volume, not an actual demand estimate. Do not combine the 12/8 daily receiving queue in Chapter 14 with a claim that every contact can be deflected safely.

A sound request is to authorize up to $12,000 for two weeks of source-bound wording, accessible receiving-route rehearsal and baseline evidence, while withholding learner exposure and the remaining $9,000 of B's hypothetical 90-day envelope until policy, Support, Security and release owners accept their respective gates. The sponsor may instead fund a narrower immediate static correction, decline the proposal, or request a larger business case. An urgent wrong live message may require authorized containment/correction now, regardless of future funding.

## Separate paper packet answer

Option M direct cost is `$9,000 + 3 × $2,000 = $15,000`. Option A is `$20,000 + 3 × $4,000 = $32,000`. Increment is `$17,000`. Upper-bound capacity is `10 tasks/day × 60 active days × 10 minutes ÷ 60 = 100 hours`; at `$40/hour` it has a planning value of `$4,000`, not cash. At all ten tasks eliminated, labor-only parity would require `17,000 × 60 ÷ (10 × 60 × 40) = 42.5 minutes/task`. If only some tasks were eligible, the threshold per eliminated task rises. The paper packet's 60 active days differ from its three-month cost horizon on purpose; name the assumption instead of quietly making them equal.

An acceptable six-line ask:

1. Decision: authorize bounded evidence and a policy-safe correction path, not broad AI exposure.
2. Outcome: people understand that receipt does not mean approval and can reach human help.
3. Options: M as simpler minimum, A as later staff-reviewed AI path, or wait with immediate containment if guidance is wrong.
4. Economics: $15,000 vs $32,000; $17,000 extra; generous labor value $4,000, so non-labor benefit requires evidence.
5. Gates: policy meaning, Support receiving capacity, access tests and release owner approval remain separate.
6. Return: bring an authorized user test, demand/queue baseline and full cost assumptions by a named date; sponsor can approve, decline or revise.

A learner may recommend M immediately if the approved static wording and route already exist. Another may recommend a two-week study before either path. A recommendation for A can be defensible if they identify a distinct material outcome, credible evidence, capacity and governance that justify it; hypothetical model speed alone is not enough.

## Transfer rubric (12 points)

Award one point each for:

1. Starts with a specific affected-person/business outcome rather than a technology purchase.
2. Names the exact sponsor decision and its actual authority boundary.
3. Gives business-as-usual/containment, do-minimum and more ambitious alternatives.
4. Separates policy, support, security and release acceptance from investment approval.
5. States time horizon and direct/lifecycle/opportunity cost distinction.
6. Shows transparent arithmetic and labels observed, assumed and invented inputs.
7. Tests one material assumption or switching value without presenting it as a forecast.
8. Accounts for non-monetized and unevenly distributed effects on people.
9. Makes a recommendation with a bounded, answerable ask and conditions.
10. Lets the sponsor say no/wait and names what happens then, including urgent containment.
11. Gives AI a permitted source/purpose, human verification and enforced permission limit.
12. Walks trigger, propagation, detection, containment, owner, correction and residual effects of a misleading decision.

## Common wrong answers and feedback

- **“The sponsor approved, so policy and Support are aligned.”** Ask for the separate policy source, receiving test, capacity acceptance and release record.
- **“$7,200 saved means $7,200 cash returns.”** Staff capacity is not cash unless costs actually fall; review and correction may consume it.
- **“The AI option fails because labor ROI is weak.”** The *labor-only* claim fails under the teaching numbers. A distinct learner benefit might justify investment once evidenced and safe.
- **“A no decision means do nothing.”** If misleading guidance exists, authorized containment and correction are independent immediate duties.

Accept a different recommendation when the learner shows the governing outcome, real alternatives, owner gates, evidence, uncertainty and an answerable sponsor decision.
