# Chapter 17: Lead Organizational Change — worked answers

All Harbor packets are invented. No Harbor training, pilot, policy approval, operational acceptance, live release or learner outcome is claimed.

## Teach-back

Adoption is sustained use of a way of working; competence and benefit require separate evidence. Attendance is only evidence that someone was present. Readiness requires policy, skill, access, capacity and owner acceptance for the *next* stage. Transition requires a receiving team that can demonstrate and accept operation, including the ability to stop and correct failure. A sponsor can set direction/resources within actual authority; other domain owners keep their policy, support, security and release decisions. Change is a feedback process, not a one-day launch.

## Worked Harbor chapter example

The invented twelve staff all attend orientation, so attendance is `12/12 = 100%`. Seven demonstrate the current-R7, wrong-draft rejection and human-route task, so demonstrated share is `7/12 × 100 ≈ 58.3%`. The five remaining need practice, access, better task design or other support; the count is not an individual rating or real workforce proficiency estimate. A safe stage remains staff-only synthetic rehearsal until named owners accept the conditions. No learner-facing exposure follows from this number.

Chapter 13's illustrative 16 mentoring/learning hours are *already excluded* from its 56 new-work hours. A candidate allocation `6 scenario design + 4 runbook review + 6 pairing/rehearsal = 16` requires the manager's approval and displaces other learning. Separately, three fictional Support staff × two hours = six Support hours; Engineering's learning reserve cannot fund or promise that work. Support service hours/capacity must be accepted by its owner. The old Chapter 14 12/day versus 8/day queue is a synthetic teaching scenario, not a measured need.

## Separate paper packet answer

Eight attended: `8/8=100%`. Five completed the scenario: `5/8=62.5%`; three have not yet demonstrated the full task. Reject the proposed live operational handover. The failure is not merely the three demonstrations: no backup, hours, capacity acceptance or independent AI stop exists. A defensible smaller path is approved static wording and current human route, if Policy, Support, Security and release owners already accept it; otherwise stay with the approved existing service while repairing the gaps. The architect may propose a supervised staff-only rehearsal, not a learner pilot.

Three acceptance conditions might be: (1) Support names an authorized primary/backup and accepts service hours, permitted context and queue-capacity evidence; (2) Operations demonstrates independent stop, approved reduced mode, event/version record and restart criteria; (3) Policy confirms current R7 source and negative case, Security verifies per-learner access, and release authority accepts the complete gate. An answer may split these differently if the decision rights remain clear.

For the three staff, offer access to current policy, one correct and one plausibly wrong synthetic case, coach/shadow practice, and a second demonstration in which they identify source, reject the wrong draft and hand off appropriately. Ask what part of the workflow made the task hard, then fix the tool or instructions as well as teaching the person. Do not publish individual scores or use the exercise as covert performance management.

Engineering decides implementation and tests within approved intent and fixes stop tooling; Policy owns R7 meaning; Support owns receiving operation/capacity; Security owns per-learner access; release authority decides exposure after evidence; sponsor resolves scope/funding. A fresh signal that may change the decision is a demonstrated trained backup, accepted support capacity, successful stop/reduced-mode rehearsal and authorized affected-person route test; a wrong R7 event changes it in the opposite direction.

## Transfer rubric (12 points)

Award one point each for:

1. Names an affected-person outcome and current human workflow before a technology adoption target.
2. Distinguishes awareness/attendance, knowledge, demonstrated ability, adoption and benefit.
3. Uses a small staff-only rehearsal before any proposed learner-facing expansion.
4. Gives role-specific synthetic cases, coaching and a backup demonstration.
5. Protects Engineering and receiving-team learning/support time without double counting.
6. Keeps current R7 meaning, staff-draft boundary and per-learner access intact.
7. Assigns policy, Support, Security, operations and release decisions to real owners.
8. Defines operational acceptance with service hours/capacity, stop, fallback, correction and restart evidence.
9. Provides a feedback-to-owner-to-decision loop with a way for dissent to change scope.
10. Uses adoption, outcome and guardrail observations without ranking people by attendance or AI use.
11. Gives AI permitted sources/purpose, human verification and enforced access/approval limits.
12. Walks a failure through trigger, propagation, detection, containment, owner, correction and residual effects.

## Common wrong answers and feedback

- **“Everyone attended, so the team is ready.”** Ask for a demonstration of the task, access and backup; only five of eight demonstrated it in the paper packet.
- **“The runbook is written, so Support now owns it.”** Ask Support to accept service hours, queue, permitted context, authority, backup and an operating drill.
- **“A higher AI-use percentage proves success.”** Ask what changed for the learner and whether wrong guidance, inaccessible paths or staff correction load changed.
- **“Resistance means people are unwilling.”** Ask what policy, tool, access, workload or affected-person problem the objection reveals.

Accept a simpler static path or a later staff-reviewed AI evaluation if the learner preserves owner gates, protected capacity and the ability to stop/revise from actual feedback.
