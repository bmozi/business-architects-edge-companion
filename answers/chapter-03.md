## Find the failed boundary

A: archived R6 is the only supplied evidence; the answer repeats it as current. The supplied-evidence/version-eligibility path is defective for the intended task. Faithful paraphrase alone does not make it a valid current answer. Inspect source status, applicability filters, ingestion/update process, and why the application permitted current-authority wording. Do not conclude that generation is flawless or that a newer model fixes the retrieval defect.

B: current R7 is supplied, but the answer introduces approval/start permission contrary to its meaning. Investigate answer support, prompt/task specification, candidate validation, and evaluation scenarios. R7 presence does not prove successful use. Preserve the separation between received, reviewed, eligible, and allowed to start.

C: abstention and use of the supplied verified support route fit the stated absence of approved evidence. This one observation does not prove the route is operationally sustainable, that the system never over-refuses, or that it performs well on other tasks. Check appropriateness and useful handling across representative cases.

D: hostile instruction influenced the proposed answer; the model's message violates the authority/meaning boundary. Lack of write capability limits a record-changing effect but does not prevent misleading a learner. Investigate source ingestion and handling, instruction/data separation, answer validation, allowed delivery, monitoring, and affected messages. Do not grant a write tool to make the proposed waiver executable. Reject/correct the answer and follow authorized incident/operational processes if appropriate. A blocked write is not proof of overall safe behavior.

Accept multiple failure contributors if supported. Do not claim one output proves a universal root cause. The expected task is an explanation, not policy determination.

## Transfer: worked Harbor recommendation

Outcome: help a learner understand the next action for one approved requirement. Alternative: reviewed deterministic message selected from authorized status, with a maintained support route. Evidence: current applicable approved R7 plus authorized status if needed; no broad private-record access. Initial generative option: explain R7 without adding a decision, with evidence links and checked output. Start with prompt/source/workflow quality rather than assume training is necessary. Fine-tuning would require a demonstrated repeatable behavior need, suitable data, cost/ownership, and evaluation.

Allowed: read the approved bounded corpus and propose an explanation. Prohibited: determine eligibility, waive requirements, write records, or independently message someone outside an authorized delivery workflow. Human owners validate policy meaning and task scope; application/service permissions enforce access and actions.

Tests: ordinary R7 request yields a supported explanation; no applicable evidence preserves uncertainty and follows an owned route; hostile instruction cannot create a waiver, unauthorized action, or misleading approved answer. Reviewer inspects generation and end-to-end behavior, not just tool logs. Fallback: maintained reviewed response or authorized human support. Pause when consequential authority/data/answer failures occur under agreed criteria; no universal numerical threshold invented.

## Self-study transfer rubric: 10 points

Outcome/non-AI alternative 2; justified evidence/intervention choice 2; permissions and decision owner 2; three cases/expected behavior 2; fallback/pause criteria 2. Ask learners to teach the difference between prompting, retrieval, and training using the same task. Correct overconfidence without penalizing a well-supported decision to avoid generation.
