# Chapter 26: Map business capabilities — worked answers

This guide accompanies the reader-facing paper exercise and transfer task in Chapter 26. Harbor Learning, the R7 rule, the dialogue, and the question packet are fictional. The packet supports arithmetic and a bounded decision; it is not a sample from a live learner population.

## Teaching purpose

The learner should use a capability map to improve a decision, rather than mistake an application inventory or organization chart for a map of business abilities. Look for an explicit outcome, stable abilities, a named human authority, evidence with a denominator, and a next action that can alter the recommendation. Allow more than one defensible priority when the reasoning is sound.

Suggested time: 30 minutes individual work, 15 minutes paired challenge, and 15 minutes discussion. The exercise also works without a group. Ask the learner to keep both the first answer and the revision after a skeptical question; the change in reasoning is part of the lesson.

## Paper exercise: worked answer

| Label in the packet | Best classification | Why, and possible ambiguity |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding team | Organization unit | A team supplies people and may own work. The ability to onboard remains even if the team is renamed or reorganized. |
| Start with clear guidance | Outcome | This describes a desired condition for the learner. Clarify what “clear” means and what “start” authorizes before measuring it. |
| Review documents | Process | As phrased, this is an activity. “Review submitted evidence for an authorized decision” could be reframed as a broader capability if it includes the people, policy, data, and control needed to do it reliably. Accept that qualified distinction. |
| CRM | Application | It may implement parts of a workflow but is not the business ability. Ask which system and use are meant before making a dependency claim. |
| Interpret status accurately | Capability | It states an ability Harbor needs despite changes in staff or systems. An acceptable alternative is “explain current status and next action accurately”; either must preserve the receipt/review distinction. |
| Buy AI assistant | Initiative | It is a proposed change or investment, not proof of an outcome or capability. |

The packet says **8 of 10 ordinary questions** have a current approved answer. That is **80% of those ten ordinary questions**. It says **2 of 4 exception questions** have no named receiving owner. That is **50% of those four exception questions**. Neither figure measures all questions, all learners, question frequency, answer quality, learner comprehension, review quality, staff workload, or successful starts. The two sets have different denominators and meanings; do not combine them into a “71% readiness” score. The counts do not establish that AI is needed, that ordinary answers are safe to send automatically, or that the owner gap caused an outcome change.

One defensible gap record is:

| Field | Example answer |
|---|---|
| Required behavior | Explain receipt as receipt, not approval; use the current approved answer; route a question that cannot be answered to a named and usable human destination. |
| Current evidence | In the invented packet, eight of ten ordinary questions have an approved answer. Two do not. Separately, two of four exceptions have no named receiving owner. No message delivery, answer correctness, learner experience, or start outcome was measured. |
| Confidence | High that the packet contains the stated labels and counts; low that they generalize to Harbor performance or explain why learners are confused. R7's rule is given for the fictional case. |
| Human owner | A policy owner approves R7 meaning; learner support and admissions operations jointly validate the next-step route and accepting team; a technical owner changes the display or tool only after approval. |
| Affected people | Admitted learners, especially those with an exception or who cannot use the default help route; support and admissions staff who receive incomplete handoffs. |
| Dependency | Current rule and approved content, permitted status fields, a receiver who accepts the case with enough context, and an accessible contact path. |
| Candidate next action | Correct any known misleading wording through the authorized owner, then review a bounded set of permitted messages and exception handoffs with affected people and staff. Name the receiver for each sampled exception type. |
| Decision informed | Whether content/route repair closes the important gap or whether a carefully bounded tool pilot deserves investment. Keep authorized staff review of satisfaction. |

The learner may instead prioritize exception routing because half of the four invented exceptions lack an owner. Reward that answer if it acknowledges the tiny, selected packet, checks whether the missing ownership recurs in actual permitted work, and preserves the distinction between an answer and a resolved case. It should still note that two ordinary answers lack an approved source, since an AI assistant cannot safely invent them. Another valid answer may start with source approval. Do not force a single ranking from evidence that does not support one.

A useful final question is: “Who is authorized to approve the meaning of a learner-facing status, and who will accept an unresolved case if the approved answer does not cover it?” Other acceptable questions seek representative cases, accessibility feedback, current rule versions, or an operational receiver's capacity. “Which model should we buy?” comes too early.

## Discussion probes and feedback

Ask, “If the CRM disappeared, which abilities would Harbor still need?” A learner who lists screens and applications should try again using verbs that survive a software swap. Ask, “What would make the two small fractions misleading?” Strong answers mention selection, unequal denominators, the absence of a time period, and the absence of outcome evidence. Ask, “What would change your recommendation?” Strong answers name observed recurrence after a simpler repair, inaccessible routes, approved-source gaps, or a measured handoff burden. Ask, “Who may say the requirement is satisfied?” The answer is authorized staff under R7, not a receipt field, AI draft, or vendor badge.

If a learner draws a full enterprise map, narrow it to the four abilities relevant to the sponsor's decision. If they call every weak-looking box a red maturity score, ask for specific evidence and confidence. If they recommend buying to avoid ownership, ask whether the product meets the rule and accessible-route requirement first; then compare total costs for viable options. If they make AI the decision-maker, ask what data it can actually see, which action is allowed, who verifies it, and what happens when it is unavailable. If they treat stakeholder dialogue as observed fact, have them label the Chapter 26 conversation as invented.

## Transfer-task scoring guide (12 points)

Award 0, 1, or 2 in each dimension. A score of 2 means the element is concrete and internally consistent; 1 means it appears but is vague or partly conflated; 0 means it is absent or contradicted.

| Dimension | What earns 2 points |
|---|---|
| Outcome and boundary | A stakeholder outcome and a bounded decision, with three or four relevant candidate abilities rather than an unbounded inventory. |
| Distinctions | Correctly separates capability from process, organization unit, application, and initiative, and explains at least one ambiguous label. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Names an exact source and denominator or observation; distinguishes the evidence from assumptions, impact, and generalization; includes one suspected weak and one stronger area without invented maturity certainty. |
| Ownership and people | Names appropriate validators and affected people, including a credible route for exceptions or accessibility needs. |
| Alternatives and AI limits | Compares a non-AI option and any AI contribution; bounds source, authority, access, verification, and work without AI. Full credit is possible when AI is rejected with a reason. |
| Reversible next step | States a decision, a bounded test or correction, a review point, and evidence that would change or stop the recommendation. |

Do not translate the total into a claim about job readiness. For feedback, identify the first missing decision link and ask the learner to revise it. A learner using a confidential workplace situation may submit a sanitized or fictionalized version; assessment should reward reasoning, not access to privileged data.

## Instructor evidence boundary

The chapter's four cited external sources support working distinctions about capabilities, processes, organizational ends and means, and AI governance responsibilities. They do not validate Harbor's map or the effectiveness of any option. The instructor should not present this fictional exercise as a pilot, a university case study, a benchmark, or evidence that AI improves outcomes.

## Information-map repair: worked answer

The packet contains a report of completed upload and a reference to a rule source. It supplies no authorized Review decision. Treat `upload_complete` according to the interface's actual receipt contract, not as a statement of satisfaction. Safer wording is: “Your upload is reported complete. That does not establish that the requirement is satisfied; an authorized review is needed.” If the interface contract does not establish receipt, narrow that wording further. Missing review data mean unknown here, not rejected or approved.

Policy resolves rule meaning; the authorized review/source owner establishes review status. Engineering maps source fields to the right concepts and enforces permitted access, but cannot infer a missing decision. Negative test: an upload-complete response without an authorized decision must never produce satisfied/approved guidance. Also challenge stale rule versions and wrong-learner association. A concept map is not a substitute for checking those controls.

Score 2 each: separate receipt/submission from review decision; identify missing fact without inventing rejection; produce bounded wording; name appropriate authority/source; give a negative technical test. Total 10. Transfer feedback rewards two concrete meanings, a consequential decision, a disconfirming example and a revised relationship; more boxes alone earn no credit.
