# Chapter 28: Choose investments under uncertainty — worked answers

The main Chapter 28 Harbor ledger and this paper exercise are **different invented cases**. Neither supplies a live rate, vendor quote, actual institutional cost, saved payroll, benefit forecast, or measured learner outcome. Require learners to show formulas and fit logic before making a recommendation.

## Purpose and pacing

The learner should reject a false equivalence before comparing prices, separate direct spending from released capacity, expose the assumptions that reverse a ranking, and propose a staged decision with accountable owners. Allow 30 minutes for individual paper work, 15 minutes for a skeptic to challenge a supposed essential requirement and a capacity claim, and 15 minutes for revision. In self-study, solve before looking below.

## Paper exercise: worked calculation

The smaller repair's direct two-year cost is **$6,000 + 2 × $2,000 = $10,000**. The staff-reviewed draft aid adds **$12,000 + 2 × $3,000 = $18,000 incremental direct cost**. If combined with the smaller repair, the stated direct cost is $28,000. Other real costs remain unknown; the exercise does not authorize a budget.

An adopted draft takes one minute of human review instead of the assumed four-minute handling on the smaller-repair path without the aid. Net potential capacity is **three minutes per adopted question**. We subtract review time once; we do not also record it as a separate dollar cost in this simplified calculation.

| Adoption | Adopted of 6,000 | Net potential capacity | Capacity-equivalent at invented $40/hour |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50% | 3,000 | 3,000 × 3/60 = **150 hours** | **$6,000** |
| 75% | 4,500 | 4,500 × 3/60 = **225 hours** | **$9,000** |

Both narrow capacity-equivalent values are less than the aid's invented $18,000 incremental direct cost. They are **not cash savings**: no budget line has been shown to fall, no staff reallocation has been agreed, and no measured time or quality result exists. Do not add $6,000 or $9,000 to a second claim about serving more learners with the same hours. The numerical exercise cannot price accessibility, truthful guidance, staff experience or authorized-start outcomes.

The $10,000 vendor demonstration tells the learner that document receipt means approval. Under R7 it **fails the essential fit test** and should not be ranked as the low-cost way to meet the same need. A vendor may return with a supported correction that passes a case-based demonstration; that would require new cost, ownership and risk estimates. Also challenge whether “must use this exact interface” is a genuine essential condition or a preferred mechanism. The policy meaning is essential; the UI design need not be.

The aid could be worth a bounded test for a nonmonetized benefit such as higher accuracy or accessibility on difficult explanations, but neither is established by this packet. It could harm people by producing an approved-sounding answer from a stale source, shifting corrections to staff, or hiding an inaccessible handoff. A sensible next action is the smaller policy-approved form/content/route repair and a staff-only evaluation **only if** a material residual need is observed. Record approved input versions, representative ordinary and exception cases, adoption, actual review/correction time, source adherence, wrong-meaning events and a manual fallback. Stop or contain the aid if it implies approval from receipt, if the receiving route fails, or if review effort wipes out the claimed capacity. Finance, policy, support, admissions and the outcome owner validate their respective claims and gates.

## Check the main Harbor worked model

In the reader's separate two-year illustration, minimum repair is `$10,000 + 2 × $4,000 = $18,000`; the noncompliant demonstrated purchase is `$8,000 + 2 × $14,000 = $36,000`; the conditional AI add-on is `$20,000 + 2 × $10,000 = $40,000 incremental`, or $58,000 with the minimum repair before exclusions. With 20,000 eligible opportunities, five comparator minutes on the minimum-repair path without AI, $50/hour and one-minute review, potential capacity-equivalent values are about $26,667 at 40% adoption, $40,000 at 60%, and $46,667 at 70%. With two-minute review, they are $20,000, $30,000 and $35,000 respectively. The one-minute-review switching adoption is 60%. At 70% adoption, review duration at the narrow $40,000 switch is `5 − (40,000 × 60)/(14,000 × 50) ≈ 1.5714 minutes`.

These are not ROI estimates. Dollar-equivalent capacity is a planning comparison only; the learner should not subtract it from payroll or assert increased starts. The figure data and automated arithmetic check reproduce each cell. An option may be chosen for a justified unmonetized benefit or rejected for policy risk despite a positive capacity cell; the calculation alone does not authorize a recommendation.

## Discussion probes and feedback

Ask: “What would you give up by funding this option?” A strong answer names another use of staff time or budget, rather than saying only “money.” Ask: “Which part is a requirement and which is a product preference?” A strong answer protects R7 and accessible resolution while remaining flexible about vendor, screen and model. Ask: “Who owns the seam if the product needs a workaround?” A strong answer names integration, testing after vendor updates, incidents and exit.

Common wrong answer 1: “The $10,000 vendor wins because it is cheapest.” Feedback: it fails R7 in the stated demonstration. First see whether a supported configuration can pass the same case tests, then recost it. A failed option is not a cheaper version of the required outcome.

Common wrong answer 2: “The aid saves $9,000 in payroll at 75% adoption.” Feedback: $9,000 is an invented capacity-equivalent value under assumptions. Payroll cash has not been released. Ask what useful work the 225 hours would actually support, whether review/correction time was measured, and which spending line would fall if claiming cash.

Common wrong answer 3: “Because the spreadsheet is negative, AI must be rejected forever.” Feedback: the narrow model omits possible quality, access and future changes. It supports staging and measurement, not a universal prohibition. Conversely, a positive optimistic cell would not prove safety or value.

Common wrong answer 4: “Review time is a direct cost and should also be subtracted from the already-net minutes.” Feedback: this double-counts review in the simplified model. A fuller cost model can price actual review labor, but then it must not also claim those minutes were freed.

## Transfer-task rubric (12 points)

Score each dimension 0, 1 or 2. Give 2 for concrete, reproducible, justified reasoning; 1 for partial or vague work; 0 for missing or contradictory work. Do not turn the total into a claim about job readiness or institutional financial performance.

| Dimension | What earns 2 points |
|---|---|
| Fit and requirement honesty | Defines essential outcome/policy/access conditions and challenges a preferred mechanism masquerading as one. |
| Alternatives and opportunity cost | Compares current, smaller and larger **viable** paths on the same scope and horizon; says what capacity or budget is displaced. |
| Arithmetic and cost boundary | Shows setup, recurring, operating and excluded costs; correct formulas; distinguishes incremental from total and capacity from cash without double-counting. |
| Uncertainty and switch | Changes at least two material assumptions, computes or explains a switching value, and says what evidence would narrow the range. |
| People and ownership | Names affected groups, decision/benefit owners and the teams or vendors that must operate, review, correct and retire the option. |
| Adaptive decision and AI limits | Gives a staged recommendation, review/stop gate, viable simpler route, and bounded AI source/authority/verification/fallback if AI is proposed. Full credit is possible without AI. |

Feedback should identify the first unjustified comparison or hidden assumption and ask for a revision. Accept sanitized or fictional transfer examples; do not require confidential employer data. The external finance guidance informs the method but does not validate any invented price, probability, capacity value or outcome.

## Quarterly portfolio exercise: worked answer

This packet is separate from the chapter's two-year model. R is required; A and B require E; C does not. Money, engineering days and policy days are different constraints.

- R+E+A+B: $6,000+$8,000+$12,000+$10,000 = **$36,000**; 2+3+5+4 = **14 engineering days**; 2+1+2+2 = **7 policy days**. This exceeds $30,000/12/6 by $6,000/2/1 respectively.
- At policy capacity **4**, the complete feasible combinations are **R** ($6,000,2,2), **R+C** ($14,000,6,2), **R+E** ($14,000,5,3), and **R+E+C** ($22,000,9,3). There are no other feasible combinations under the stipulated dependencies and mandatory R. Every A/B combination needs at least R+E plus that component, or 5 policy days.
- R+E+B is a defensible candidate only under the original capacity and the stipulated preference for cross-channel consistency: **$24,000,9,5**. It is not a mathematically unique best choice.

A valid reduced-capacity recommendation is R+C if the preference problem merits action and C's acceptance remains valid, or R now while discretionary evidence is gathered. R+E can be justified by a credible later consumer or independent value, but E is not automatically worth funding. R+E+C spends $8,000 and 3 engineering/1 policy days more than R+C; identify what that additional investment buys before preferring it. Reserving budget is legitimate.

A and B might both claim the same repeat contacts avoided for the same population/period. Keep the comparator and benefit owner explicit; do not add overlapping projected saved hours. Charge E once in a union. If each consumer needs distinct integration, keep those real costs separate rather than assuming sharing makes them free. A ranked list is not a feasible portfolio.

Score 2 each for three-resource arithmetic; mandatory/dependency closure; feasible reduced-capacity combinations; strategy/evidence-aware choice; single counting of shared costs/benefits; revised authority/sequence and deferred consequence. Total 12. Common errors: leave out E, fund E twice, substitute spare money for unavailable Policy, cut a review day without a changed scope/acceptance, or declare the largest feasible spend the optimum. The offline checker enumerates the entire finite packet but supplies no strategy ranking.
