# Chapter 30: Measure Business and Engineering Value — worked answers

## Paper answer
Ordinary comprehension: 40/80 = 50% before, 54/80 = 67.5% after. Exception comprehension: 10/20 = 50% before, 8/20 = 40% after. Handoff acceptance: 24/30 = 80% before, 25/45 = 55.6% after. Overall comprehension: 50/100 = 50%, 62/100 = 62%, but that aggregate obscures the exception decline. Deployment count rises 36 to 60 but measures release activity. Denominator trap: report 25 accepted handoffs as an improvement over 24 while hiding the growth from 30 to 45 attempts, or infer one person's learning from two different samples. Guardrails: wrong receipt-as-satisfaction claim, unresolved exception burden, inaccessible route, privacy, review load. Recommendation: investigate why exception understanding and acceptance fell; keep current safe wording/human route, do not expand AI based on the aggregate. No causal or start-rate claim.

## Teach-back and transfer rubric (12 points)
Award 2 each for an explicit business outcome and affected population; operational definitions with numerator/denominator/period; at least one leading and one guardrail measure; ownership and data source; attribution/confounder limit; decision/reversal tied to evidence. A simpler non-AI intervention can earn full credit. Common errors: calling deployments the business outcome (ask whose situation changed); calling saved minutes cash (ask what budget actually changed); claiming 12-point rise caused starts (ask for comparison and outcome observation). Feedback should praise a precise unknown rather than reward invented certainty.
