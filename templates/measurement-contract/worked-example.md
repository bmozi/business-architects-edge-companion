# Measurement Contract: fictional worked example

From reader Chapter 30. Define populations, numerator/denominator or observation, source, cadence and limits. These are proposed or illustrative Harbor records, not actual agreements or outcomes.

| Question | Proposed definition | Evidence owner and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Do people understand the next step? | Among consenting participants who received an R7 receipt explanation in the period, numerator = those who can correctly state “received for review; authorized review pending” and identify an accepted next action; denominator = all sampled eligible participants asked the same question. Report sample size, language, accessibility mode and nonresponse. | Learner research/support; a quiz response is a proxy for understanding, not a successful start. |
| Is the route usable? | Count attempted handoffs, received by an authorized team, returned for missing context, and unresolved beyond an agreed time window, by channel and accessibility need. | Support operations; HTTP 202 or ticket creation is not resolution. |
| Does R7 meaning stay intact? | Review a defined sample of displayed and drafted explanations against current approved passage; record wrong “satisfied” claims and policy version. | Policy owner validates meaning; Engineering can test exact negative cases, not approve policy. |
| Does delivery remain safe? | Track change lead time and deployment rework with failed-change/recovery evidence; count rollback/correction burden. | Platform/release owners; DORA-style delivery measures diagnose the delivery system, not learner value. |
| What does it cost to sustain? | Record staff review, correction, support, maintenance, vendor/API, accessibility and outage time alongside any capacity released. | Finance and functional owners; saved minutes are capacity until a real budget or workload change occurs. |
