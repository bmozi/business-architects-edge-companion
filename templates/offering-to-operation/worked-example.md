# Offering To Operation: fictional worked example

From reader Chapter 27. Connect a fictional customer offer and its economics to sustainable operating capabilities. These are proposed or illustrative teaching records, not actual agreements or outcomes.

| Question | Candidate subscription and the decision it creates |
|---|---|
| Who values what? | Small-shop owners may value predictable maintenance and a dependable response. Validate which equipment, hours and response matter before promising them. |
| How does the offer reach them? | Existing account contacts and a phone/web request route. An online-only route could exclude customers who need phone access. |
| What sustains it? | An annual fee must support planned work, unplanned demand, administration, parts and continuing capacity. Price and renewal intent are untested. |
| What must the business be able to do? | Verify service entitlement, plan preventive visits, diagnose faults, dispatch qualified people and resolve supplier-dependent repairs. An assistant supports parts of this work. |
| Which external contribution matters? | A parts supplier and any subcontractor must accept availability, coverage and escalation terms; appearing in a supplier list is insufficient. |
| What would change the decision? | Demand exceeding accepted technician capacity, unaffordable call-outs or an unreliable parts route could favor a bounded plan or the improved existing service. |
